Research

I did not begin college as a particularly academic student, and I joined Prof. Kim's laboratory without a clear plan, simply to see what research was like. What changed me was testing a hypothesis of my own: that swapping a single component of an ionic liquid could predictably tune how a transistor switches. Designing the experiments, questioning each result, and finally seeing the idea confirmed by data I had measured myself showed me that what I truly enjoy is solving a scientific problem.

I study how the chemical structure of a conjugated polymer, its counterion, and its surrounding electrolyte set the physical and electronic behavior of organic mixed ionic–electronic conductors (OMIECs). I approach this question from two sides: building the material at the monomer stage, and controlling its doping through the electrolyte of a working device.

Setting the energy levels of OMIECs at the monomer stage

M.S. research, 2024–present  ·  Manuscript in preparation

Question. Can the energy levels of an OMIEC be set before the material is ever made, simply by choosing one unit in its monomer?

Approach. Keeping EDOT at both ends of a donor–acceptor–donor (D-A-D) monomer, I used Stille coupling to synthesize a series in which only the central unit changes: thiophene and bithiophene serve as references with essentially zero accepting strength, while BDT, NDI, DPP, and BBT span a range of electron-accepting strengths. Because the α-positions of EDOT are highly reactive, every monomer polymerizes oxidatively, carrying the chosen core directly into the backbone without a separate polymer synthesis for each target.

Polymerization. I first screened the series by PSS-templated polymerization in water, following the route used to make PEDOT:PSS, and then moved to surface oxidative polymerization with iron(III) tosylate, which grows a tosylate-doped OMIEC film directly on a substrate in a single step. Adding a base slowed the otherwise rapid reaction during spin-coating.

Analysis. Combining UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy, cyclic voltammetry, and TD-DFT calculations (ORCA), I relate the accepting strength of the core to the HOMO and LUMO levels of both the monomers and their polymers.

Tuning OECT switching through the molecular design of the electrolyte

Undergraduate and M.S. research, 2022–present  ·  One manuscript under review and one in preparation

Enhancement-mode operation. PEDOT:PSS is heavily doped as cast, so turning an OECT off at zero gate bias requires the electrolyte to do more than carry ions. I studied why the protic ionic liquid [DBUH][OAc] allows a solid-electrolyte PEDOT:PSS transistor to operate in enhancement mode while the common [EMIM][TFSI] does not. XPS, DFT, and capacitance–voltage measurements showed that [DBUH][OAc] interacts preferentially with the ion-conduction pathway of PEDOT:PSS, giving the channel the accumulation and depletion profile of an organic semiconductor rather than a degenerate conductor.

Tuning by chemistry alone. In a DBU-based acid–base ionic liquid (ABIL), the extent of proton transfer, and therefore how much neutral base remains, depends on the pKa of the acid. I synthesized a series of ABILs from DBU and carboxylic acids of different pKa and quantified their ionicity by ¹H NMR and DFT. Changing only the anion shifted the threshold voltage of PEDOT:PSS OECTs by up to 500 mV.

Mechanism. Because a Raman shift alone could not prove de-doping, I combined UV-Vis-NIR, Raman, XPS, GIWAXS, cyclic voltammetry, and EIS-based capacitance–voltage analysis. Together they supported a single causal chain: neutral DBU reduces PEDOT through single-electron transfer, lowering the hole density and shifting the threshold voltage.

Circuits. I used this tunability to build zero-VGS-load OECT inverters whose switching threshold follows the ABIL composition.

Other projects

Ion storage layers for electrochromic displays — collaboration with Prof. Inho Song's group on the morphology and crystallinity of transparent capacitive ion storage layers (ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces, accepted).

Long-term memory organic transistors — bilayer electrolyte/ferroelectric-polymer gate dielectrics that retain synaptic states for more than 24 hours. Although P–E hysteresis first pointed to ferroelectric polarization, control experiments without the ferroelectric polymer traced the memory to the high ionic resistance of a low-ion-concentration electrolyte.

Methods

  • Synthesis: Pd-catalyzed Stille coupling, oxidative polymerization (solution and surface), protic ionic liquid synthesis, column chromatography
  • Devices: OECT fabrication and figure-of-merit extraction (gm, VT, on/off, μC*, transient response); OECT inverters
  • Electrochemistry: CV, EIS, EIS-based C–V, spectroelectrochemistry
  • Characterization: XPS, Raman, UV-Vis-NIR, FT-IR, NMR, GIWAXS, AFM, TGA, DSC
  • Computation: DFT/TD-DFT (ORCA, Spartan), Python, MATLAB
